A Polycentric World Cinema

Shohat & Stam, Introduction
World Cinema – Fall 2026

A Polycentric World

  • The world has many dynamic cultural locations and many possible vantage points.
  • Polycentrism introduces a principle of differentiation, relationality, and linkage.
  • No single community, regardless of economic or political power, should be epistemologically privileged as the universal center.

Polycentric / Polycultural Multiculturalism

  • Goes beyond sensitivity or tolerance toward other groups.
  • Aims to empower the disempowered and transform oppressive institutions and discourses.
  • Challenges us to ask whose stories are told, from whose perspective, and who benefits from existing arrangements.

Sorting Exercise: Multicultural / Polycentric / Still Eurocentric

  • In groups, classify examples from campus, streaming platforms, and film festivals into three categories:
  • Multicultural – many cultures visible, some redistribution of representation.
  • Polycentric / Polycultural – multiple centers of narration and meaning; no default vantage point.
  • Still Eurocentric – other cultures present but organized around a single dominant center.

Clip Setup: Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom

  • Dinner at Pankot Palace: Jones, Willie, Short Round, the Maharaja, British officer, and Indian hosts.
  • The village believes an evil force has taken their children; Jones suspects a revived Thuggee cult.
  • We use this familiar scene to test the difference between multicultural representation and Eurocentric narrative framing.

Viewing Prompts

  • Where does the scene appear multicultural (many cultures on screen)?
  • Whose vantage point defines what counts as normal vs exotic, civilized vs primitive?
  • Which characters possess knowledge, agency, and interpretive authority in the scene?

From Multicultural to Polycentric: Rewriting a Moment

  • Identify a moment that is clearly multicultural but not polycultural (many cultures, one narrative center).
  • Imagine how you would change that moment to reflect polycentric multiculturalism.
  • Consider shifts in point of view, dialogue, camera work, and who gets to interpret events.

Exit Reflection

  • Option 1: Name one way today changed how you understand the relationship between multiculturalism and polycentric World Cinema.
  • Option 2: Choose one moment from the Temple of Doom clip and describe how you would revise it to reflect polycentric multiculturalism.
  • Use at least one key term: Eurocentrism, multiculturalism, polycentric, colonialism, racism, or DEI.
